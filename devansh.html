The one problem worth solving early: you train at night, you race in the morning

Every session at 19:00 for seven weeks trains your body to be at its best in the evening — body temperature, hormones, reaction time, perceived effort all shift to match your schedule. Then on Sunday 20 September you'll be asked to race hard at roughly 08:00, at a time your body has spent two months learning is for sleeping. That gap is worth somewhere between two and five percent of your performance, and on a 90-minute race that's real minutes.

  • Weeks 1–2: keep training at 19:00. Only the Saturday session moves early.
  • Weeks 3–5: every session marked MORNING moves early — that's the Saturday simulations and one weekday run. This is also when the shared sessions with CB begin.
  • Week 6: move as many sessions to the morning as your schedule allows. Two weeks of morning training blunts most of the penalty.
  • Week 7: wake at race-day wake time every single morning, including the rest days. Especially the rest days.

The other half of evening training is sleep. Finish by 20:30, no caffeine after 15:00, shake within 20 minutes, dinner by 21:15. A 18:30 pre-workout coffee is the most common reason evening athletes lie awake at midnight wondering why.

You and CB are not on the same plan, and shouldn't be

He's coming off three months completely detrained, with a stitched right middle finger that rules out grip until mid-August and a shoulder that flags on the SkiErg. You're somewhat active, uninjured, and can train fully from day one. So for the first fortnight you'll be well ahead of him — don't pace off him, and don't wait.
