Week 1 — Re-entry (No Grip)
03 Aug – 09 Aug

Right middle finger is out of action and there are no leg machines — so this week is bodyweight, the track, and nothing else. That sounds like a limitation. For a detrained Doubles athlete in week one, it is very close to the correct programme anyway.

Mon 03 Aug
Bodyweight Legs & Core
Moderate 60 min ▾
Training — Tempo and unilateral work
Shoulder Protocol — NO-GRIP version, 8 min
  • Loop a light resistance band around both WRISTS, not your hands. Your fingers do nothing.
  • Band pull-apart at the wrists — 2 x 20▶
  • Band external rotation, band looped over the wrist, elbow pinned to side — 2 x 15/arm▶
  • Prone Y-T-W on the floor — 2 x 8 each letter, no weight, open hands, thumbs up▶
  • Scapular wall slides, forearms on the wall — 2 x 10▶
  • Standing scapular retraction, arms by sides — 2 x 15▶
Main — 3 rounds, rest 90s between rounds. Nothing touches your right hand.
  • Tempo air squat — 3 x 12 (3 seconds down, 1 second pause at the bottom, stand up normally)▶
  • Bodyweight reverse lunge — 3 x 10/leg▶
  • Bulgarian split squat, rear foot on a bench — 3 x 8/leg (steady yourself with your LEFT hand only if you need to)▶
  • Single-leg glute bridge, shoulders on the floor — 3 x 12/leg▶
  • Slider hamstring curl (heels on a towel, hips up, drag the heels in) — 3 x 10▶
  • Single-leg calf raise off a step — 3 x 15/leg▶
Core — no hand loading
  • Forearm plank — 3 x 45s (forearms, never palms)▶
  • Dead bug — 3 x 10/side▶
  • Bird dog, on forearm — 3 x 10/side▶
  • Lying leg raise — 3 x 12▶
  • Side plank on forearm — 3 x 30s/side▶
The rule for the next two weeks
  • If a movement asks your right hand to grip, hang, press into the floor, or take any weight at all — skip it. Nothing here is worth reopening stitches for.
Why tempo instead of load
  • With no machines and no grip, you can’t add weight — so you add time under tension instead. A 3-second descent on an air squat is genuinely hard by round three, and for someone three months detrained it is more than enough stimulus.▶

TRAVEL DAY — Departure
Travel 20 min ▾
Training — Move, don't train
Today is a write-off, and that is completely fine
  • You are four days into a seven-week block. Missing two easy sessions in Week 1 costs you nothing measurable. Travelling badly and arriving inflamed and dehydrated costs you the following week.
If you have 20 minutes at any point
  • 10 min brisk walk — airport terminal, station platform, hotel corridor, anywhere
  • Couch stretch 2 x 60s/side and 90/90 hip switches 2 x 10 — do these in the room, not on the plane▶
  • Forearm plank 3 x 45s and dead bug 3 x 10/side if the floor is clean▶
Travel hydration — the thing that actually matters today
  • 500 ml water for every 2 hours of travel, plus one electrolyte tab across the day
  • Sealed or filtered water only. A stomach bug this week would cost you far more than the missed sessions.
  • Get up and walk every hour on any journey over three hours
Your hand
  • Keep the wound dry and covered in transit. Pack a spare dressing. No pools, no sea, no beach cricket — salt water and sand are exactly what stitches don't need at day 5.

Right middle finger — how the first three weeks change

Stitches mean no grip, no hand weight-bearing and no punching until roughly 16 August. Rather than water down the original plan, Weeks 1 and 2 have been rewritten from scratch as genuinely hands-free training — and here is the part that matters: this costs you almost nothing. The six things that actually matter

You have 49 days, not 40

Aug 3 to Sept 20 is exactly seven weeks starting tomorrow. That extra week is the difference between a rushed build and a proper one — it buys you a full re-entry week so your tendons don't file a complaint in Week 3.

Doubles changes everything about what to train

Stations get split with your partner — roughly half the work. The running does not. Both of you run all eight kilometres, side by side, within 15 seconds of each other. So the 8 km is 100% of an individual race while the stations are ~50%. Running is your highest-leverage training, by a distance.

Your shoulder is a smaller problem than you think

SkiErg is 1000 m split between two people. If your partner takes 600 m and you break your 400 m into two blocks, you'll barely touch the thing. Meanwhile the shoulder protocol runs all seven weeks and the boxing bag builds the exact endurance you need without loading the joint the way a SkiErg does.

The 150 kg push is genuinely good news

Sled push at 152 kg is where most people's races die. You already move it. In Doubles you'll switch every 12.5 m, meaning ~25 m of work total. Your strength survived the layoff; your engine didn't. Train the engine.

Week 1 must feel too easy

Three months off plus a hard simulation is the classic setup for a Week 2 injury — and the stitched finger enforces that restraint for you. Nothing above RPE 7. If you finish Week 1 feeling like you sandbagged it, it worked.

Sleep is the ceiling on everything else here

At 01:00 to 06:15 you were looking at roughly five hours — below the point where training stops producing adaptation and starts producing injuries. Moving to 23:30 buys you close to seven, and it will do more for your race than any session in this plan. That's not a throwaway line: on a seven-week rebuild off three months detrained, recovery is the scarce resource, not training time.

No machines is barely a constraint

You cannot add load without machines, so the plan adds time under tension instead — 3-second descents, 1.5-reps, pauses, unilateral work. For someone three months detrained, bodyweight at a hard tempo is more than enough stimulus for two weeks. Hills replace the sled better than any machine would have, and the two stations that actually need load, sled push and farmers, come back in Week 3 with real weight.

The finger costs you less than you'd think

Two weeks without grip removes carries, burpees, bag work and barbells from your hands. It removes nothing at all from your running — and in Doubles you run every metre of the 8 km yourself. The stations you're missing are the ones you split with a partner. The training you can still do is the training that matters most.

Nothing new on race day

Every gel, every shoe, every breakfast gets tested in the Week 4 and Week 5 simulations. Race day is the day you repeat what you've already done.

Shoulder protocol

Shoulder Protocol A — do this first, 8 min

  • Band pull-apart — 2 x 20 (slow, squeeze shoulder blades)
  • Band external rotation, elbow pinned to side — 2 x 15/arm
  • Prone Y-T-W on floor or bench — 2 x 8 each letter, no weight
  • Scapular pull-ups (hang, shrug down only) — 2 x 8
  • Wall slides with band — 2 x 10

Shoulder Protocol B — do this at the end, 6 min

  • Face pulls — 3 x 15
  • Bottoms-up KB/DB carry — 3 x 20m each arm (light)
  • Doorway pec stretch — 3 x 30s
  • Lat stretch on bench — 3 x 30s

Supplements

Ordered by how much difference they'll actually make. The first two do most of the work; the rest cover gaps that a no-egg vegetarian diet genuinely leaves open. None of this is medical advice — if you're on medication or have a condition, check with a doctor first.

WhatDoseWhy it's on the list
Creatine monohydrate5 g daily, any time, foreverThe single highest-value supplement for a vegetarian athlete. Vegetarians start with lower muscle creatine stores, so the response is larger than it would be for a meat-eater. Expect a real gain in repeat-sprint and sled work.

How the fuelling plan is built

Three day-types, not one diet

Hard days run ~3,100 kcal with 400–430 g carbs. Moderate days ~2,800 kcal. Easy and rest days ~2,500 kcal with carbs cut to ~250 g. Protein holds steady near 150–175 g every single day — carbs fuel the work, protein repairs it, and you never stop repairing.

Built for ~75 kg

If you're heavier or lighter, adjust the grains — rice, roti, oats — by roughly 1.5% per kilo away from 75 kg, and leave the protein portions alone. If you're 85 kg, that's about 15% more rice and the same paneer.

Menus rotate, they don't repeat daily

Six hard-day menus, five moderate, four easy, cycling through the seven weeks. You'll never eat the same thing two days running, but the shopping list stays short enough to actually follow.

The no-egg problem, solved

Hitting 150 g of protein without eggs is the real constraint here. The answer is soya chunks (52 g per 100 g dry), paneer, curd, milk, dal and one or two whey shakes. Every day on this plan clears 150 g without you having to think about it.

Iron and B12 need active management

Non-heme iron absorbs poorly. Lemon on the rajma, orange with the seeds, and no chai or coffee within an hour of an iron-heavy meal. B12 comes from dairy alone on this plan, which isn't enough for a training athlete — hence the supplement.

Race week is its own thing

Fibre drops from Thursday. Carb load runs Friday and Saturday at ~7 g/kg. Race morning is a breakfast you've already eaten before a simulation. Mumbai in September is humid — electrolytes from Thursday onward, not just on the day.

Doubles race strategy

The rules that shape everything

  • Both partners run all 8 x 1 km. No splitting the runs.
  • You must stay within 15 seconds of each other — the slower runner sets the pace, so run together from the gun.
  • Station work splits any way you like. It does not have to be 50/50.
  • Only one partner works at a time on the machines and the sled.
  • The Roxzone is race time. The clock never stops.

Where you take the bigger share

  • Sled push — your strength. Take more.
  • Sled pull — if the shoulder holds up, take half.
  • Farmers carry — grip is trainable and you've been carrying since Week 1.
  • Wall balls — alternate every 10, never go to failure.

Where you take the smaller share

  • SkiErg — ask your partner for 600 m if they'll take it, and break your 400 m into two blocks.
  • Row — only if the shoulder is complaining by then; otherwise split it evenly, the row is mostly legs.

Free minutes most teams throw away

  • Jog the Roxzone instead of walking it — worth 3–5 minutes across eight transitions.
  • Practise the hand-off so nobody stands still during a switch.
  • Switch before you're blown, not after.
  • Go out slow on run 1. Everyone around you won't, and you'll pass most of them by run 6.
